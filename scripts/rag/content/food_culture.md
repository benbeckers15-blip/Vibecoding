Tasmanian food culture and wine culture are genuinely inseparable. The island produces some of Australia's finest oysters — Bruny Island and Freycinet in particular — alongside Atlantic salmon, abalone, grassfed beef, and a cheese industry that has matured considerably in the past decade. Farmers markets run in Hobart on Saturdays at Salamanca Place and in Launceston on Sundays, and both are worth building a morning around before heading to cellar doors. Many wineries now operate kitchens or maintain relationships with local providores for cheese and charcuterie platters — this is worth checking before you visit, as a good platter mid-tasting changes the pace of the day entirely. The broader food scene in Hobart, anchored by the Salamanca precinct and an increasingly serious restaurant culture in the CBD, means evenings after a day of tastings are well catered for. Don't overlook lunch at the winery itself where it's offered — several cellar door kitchens in the Coal River Valley and Tamar operate at a level that warrants a dedicated booking.
