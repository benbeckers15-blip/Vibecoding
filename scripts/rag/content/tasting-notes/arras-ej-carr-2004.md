# Tasting note — House of Arras EJ Carr Late Disgorged 2004 (Pipers River)

The EJ Carr Late Disgorged 2004 from House of Arras is one of the most ambitious sparkling wines made anywhere in Australia, and the case for it sits in the glass within seconds. The mousse is fine, almost silken — a slow, persistent bead that climbs the side of the glass rather than crowding the surface — and the nose carries the unmistakable signature of a wine that has spent more than fifteen years on lees: toasted brioche, browned butter, roasted hazelnut, dried fig, and a high citrus note that lifts the whole thing back to brightness before it settles. On the palate the wine is dense without being heavy, structured by the kind of fine, mouth-coating acidity that only cool-climate Tasmanian fruit produces, with a savoury core of mushroom and almond skin running underneath the orchard fruit. The dosage is restrained — the wine reads as bone-dry — and the finish is improbably long, holding citrus zest and biscuit for the better part of a minute. Made in the traditional method from Chardonnay and Pinot Noir grown in Pipers River, the blend was aged on lees for over fifteen years before disgorging in 2019, which is what gives it the autolytic complexity that distinguishes it from younger Arras releases. It is a wine that asks to be drunk slowly, ideally with something quietly luxurious — oysters, raw scallops, aged Comté, or nothing at all on a clear evening. Production is small and the wine is best sought at the cellar door or on allocation. It rewards patience in the glass and in the cellar both.
