# Tasting note — Holm Oak 'The Wizard' Riesling 2023 (Tamar Valley)

The Wizard from Holm Oak is the kind of bone-dry Tasmanian riesling that argues, quietly but firmly, that the Tamar Valley belongs in conversation with the Eden and Clare for serious cool-climate examples of the variety. The wine is pale straw with a faint green-gold edge, and the nose is almost severe in its purity: kaffir lime leaf, white grapefruit pith, crushed slate, jasmine, and a thread of something like wet stone that runs through everything else without dominating. There is no oak, no malolactic, no residual sugar to soften the line — the wine is built on acid and minerality alone, and that structural honesty is its whole argument. On the palate the attack is racy and immediate, with green apple and citrus rind cut by a knife-edge of acidity that pulls the wine forward across the tongue rather than letting it pool. The mid-palate is taut, almost austere in its first year, but the texture builds with air, taking on a faint waxy quality that hints at what bottle age will do. The finish is dry, long, and stony, with a saline echo that lingers. Fermented in stainless steel from single-vineyard fruit on the Tamar's schist soils, this is a riesling that cellars well — five to ten years will bring out the kerosene and honeyed notes the variety is famous for, but the wine drinks beautifully now alongside fresh oysters, Atlantic salmon, or a Vietnamese herb salad with green chilli. Serve it cold enough that the acid does the work. Older vintages drink for less money than the quality suggests.
