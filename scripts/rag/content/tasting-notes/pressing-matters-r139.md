# Tasting note — Pressing Matters R139 Botrytis Riesling 2021 (Coal River Valley)

The R139 from Pressing Matters is a botrytised riesling in the late-harvest tradition, and it is one of the most distinctive sweet wines made in Australia. The number references the wine's residual sugar — roughly 139 grams per litre — which puts it firmly in trockenbeerenauslese territory, balanced against the kind of bracing acid that only Tasmanian riesling delivers. The colour is deep gold with an amber edge, almost glowing, and the nose is opulent without ever tipping into excess: dried apricot, candied orange peel, honeycomb, beeswax, saffron, ginger, and the unmistakable funky-sweet note of noble rot running underneath. On the palate the wine is unctuous, syrupy in texture, coating the tongue with concentrated stone fruit and quince paste, but the acid arrives before the sweetness has time to cloy, cutting through the wine and pulling it clean off the palate. The finish is exceptionally long — orange marmalade, dried mango, a faint resinous edge — and the wine drinks far less heavily than its sugar content suggests. Made from late-harvested riesling fruit deliberately exposed to noble rot in the cool, damp microclimate of the Coal River Valley, the wine is small-production, vintage-dependent, and not made every year. It pairs beautifully with blue cheese, foie gras, salted caramel desserts, or — most simply — a wedge of comté and nothing else. Cellar it for ten or fifteen years and the resinous, marmalade notes will deepen further. This is a wine for the end of a meal, the end of an evening, or the kind of celebration that warrants a half-bottle pour and no rush.
