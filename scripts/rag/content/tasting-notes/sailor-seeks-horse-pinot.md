# Tasting note — Sailor Seeks Horse Pinot Noir 2022 (Huon Valley)

Sailor Seeks Horse makes the kind of pinot noir that rewards close attention rather than demanding it, and the 2022 is a quiet, deeply considered wine from the Huon Valley's cool fringe. The colour is mid-ruby with a translucent rim — a deliberate restraint that signals the gentle hand at work in the cellar. The nose is layered and slow to open: red cherry, pomegranate, dried thyme, beetroot leaf, forest floor, and a faint spice from a measured proportion of whole-bunch fermentation. On the palate the wine is savoury rather than fruit-forward, with fine, dusty tannin running underneath bright red fruit and a thread of acidity that keeps everything in balance. There is no heaviness, no overt oak, no extracted concentration trying to compensate for site limitations — the fruit comes from a vineyard that sits at the southern edge of viable viticulture, and the winemaking trusts the place to speak for itself. The mid-palate has weight without volume, and the finish runs long on tea-leaf, sour cherry, and a stony minerality that pulls the wine back to the earth. Hand-harvested, wild-fermented, lightly extracted, and aged in seasoned French oak with minimal racking, this is a pinot built in the modern Burgundian register but rooted unmistakably in Tasmania. It drinks well now with duck, mushroom risotto, or a simple roast chicken, but rewards another three to five years in bottle. Paul and Gilli Lipscombe — the producers — are part of a generation of southern Tasmanian winemakers redefining what cool-climate pinot can be on this island.
