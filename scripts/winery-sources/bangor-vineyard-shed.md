## Founding & History

Bangor is an historic farming property spanning approximately 6,000 to 6,200 hectares, located at Dunalley in the cool, south-east corner of Tasmania. The Dunbabin family has farmed the property since 1890, establishing it as one of Tasmania's oldest continually run family farms. The broader Bangor Estate's history traces back to 1830 when John Dunbabin, a convicted horse thief, earned his freedom and purchased land, laying the groundwork for five generations of farming. In the 1830s, the property was developed to supply food, particularly beef, to the penal settlement at Port Arthur.

The venture into viticulture began in 2010 with the planting of the first vines, including Pinot Noir, Pinot Gris, and Chardonnay, selected for their suitability to Tasmania's cool maritime climate. In 2013, approximately 2,000 hectares of Bangor were affected by bushfires that devastated the local area of Dunalley, resulting in the destruction of the shed intended for cellar door operations. A new, purpose-built structure, the Bangor Vineyard Shed, was subsequently constructed on the same site and officially opened on December 1, 2014. From its inception in 2014 until June 2017, the establishment was known as the Bangor Wine & Oyster Shed before being renamed the Bangor Vineyard Shed. Since its opening, both the cellar door and the vineyard have expanded in size.

## Vineyard & Site

Bangor Vineyard Shed is situated at Dunalley, in the cool, south-east corner of Tasmania, on the Forestier Peninsula. The total property encompasses approximately 6,000 to 6,200 hectares.
