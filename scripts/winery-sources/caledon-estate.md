## Founding & History
Caledon Estate was established in 2019 when owners James and Karen Stewart planted their first vines on a former sheep farm in Richmond, Tasmania. The couple, both Tasmanian natives, returned home in 2016 after living in Sydney, Melbourne, and Perth for 16 years, with the aspiration of creating a vineyard and a family home. They purchased the land in late 2018. The site, previously known as "misery farm" by an old farm manager due to the difficulty of growing anything there, was transformed into a vineyard. The cellar door, architecturally designed by Cumulus Studio and built with sandstone excavated from the site, began construction in early 2022 and opened to the public in January 2025. Their first vintage was in 2022, and by 2023, they had bottled their full range of wines.

## People
Caledon Estate is owned by James and Karen Stewart, a family who moved back to Tasmania in 2016. James Stewart is a corporate lawyer, and Karen Stewart has a farming background. The vineyard manager is Justin Folloso, who was "blown away" by the site's potential for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay. Winemaking for the Caledon Estate range is handled by Matt Wood of Spring Vale Vineyard. Matt Wood returned to Spring Vale in 2022 and promptly made Caledon's first vintage, focusing on wines that reflect the site's uniqueness.

## Vineyard & Site
The vineyard spans 11.27 hectares (11ha) and is situated on a steep, exposed property in Tasmania's Coal River Valley. Its elevation ranges from 30 metres above sea level at its lowest point to 140 metres at its highest. The site generally has a westerly aspect and battles prevailing north-westerly winds. The soils are described as shallow and rocky, with geology changing significantly with elevation. At the base, Riesling, Sauvignon Blanc, and sparkling varieties grow on 800 to 1,000 millimetres of lighter clay loam over sandy loam, with sandstone beneath. A Pinot Gris block at 80 metres elevation sits on 300 to 500 millimetres of red to brown clay over sandstone. The main Pinot Noir blocks and some Chardonnay are found at 140 metres, supported by 500 to 700 millimetres of brown to black clay over dolerite. The vineyard's soils are generally 300 million-year-old dolerite-derived soils. The vineyard enjoys a northerly aspect and is supplied with fresh water from the Derwent River via the Tasmanian Irrigation Scheme.

Varietals planted include Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Gris, Riesling, and a small planting of Cabernet Sauvignon. The vines, which were six years old as of the source publication, grow on their own roots and were sourced mainly from cuttings from Spring Vale, Craigie Knowe, and Boomer Creek in Tasmania. The vineyard was farmed conventionally for its first five years under previous manager Josh Winen. The vineyard blocks are carved out among native gum trees, with only one tree removed during planting. Paddocks are lightly grazed by sheep to maintain natural ground cover.

## Cellar Door Experience
The cellar door at Caledon Estate is open Thursday to Monday from 10 am to 5 pm. It is closed on Christmas Day, Boxing Day, and Good Friday. Bookings are recommended, especially for larger groups or special events, but walk-ins are welcome where availability permits.

The architecturally designed cellar door offers panoramic views over the Coal River Valley towards kunanyi/Mt Wellington. It features a five-metre-high stone wall made from sandstone excavated from the site, an open fireplace, and upholstered bench seating. Visitors can enjoy guided wine tastings, purchase wines by the glass or bottle, and dine from a wine bar menu.

Tastings are offered in two formats:
* The "High Five Wine Tasting" is self-guided, includes five Estate wines, and costs $20 per person.
* The "Complete Collection Wine Tasting" is a hosted, in-depth tasting of the entire wine range (currently nine wines) for $35 per person, taking between 30-45 minutes.
Tasting fees are waived with any bottle purchase. The cellar door is described as a "stunning, cosy space" and a "welcoming and inclusive comfort zone".

## Food & Hospitality
Caledon Estate offers a wine bar-inspired food menu from Thursday to Monday, featuring dishes designed for sharing and utilizing local Tasmanian produce. The menu includes items like oysters and local lamb. For groups of six or more, a "Feed Me" menu is available, offering a range of dishes for $45 per person, or a "Feed Me More" option for $60 per person. The winery can cater to dietary restrictions. Private functions, celebrations, and weddings can also be hosted, with areas of the cellar door and vineyard available for reservation.

## Awards & Recognition
Caledon Estate's Reserve Pinot Noir 2022 received 93 points (silver) from Halliday Wine Companion, and their Pinot Gris 2024 also received 93 points (silver). The Caledon Rosé 2023 received 91 points (silver).

## Sustainability & Certifications
Caledon Estate emphasizes a commitment to sustainability, viewing it as integral to crafting their wines. They act as conscious stewards of the land, acknowledging the wisdom of the palawa people and aiming for ecological harmony. Sustainable viticulture practices include protecting soil health through the use of organic sprays, maintaining native grasses, and minimal tillage to reduce erosion and support beneficial organisms. They also encourage biodiversity with corridors and wildlife-friendly practices to control pests, reducing reliance on chemical interventions. Environmental stewardship extends to water and energy management, utilizing drip irrigation, rainwater harvesting, and soil moisture monitoring. The use of hydro-electricity and energy-conserving machinery minimizes emissions. The vineyard was planted around existing native gum trees, with minimal land disturbance.

## Notable / Distinctive
Caledon Estate is notable for its challenging and uncompromising site, with a vineyard that climbs significantly in elevation and features diverse, shallow, rocky soils over sandstone and dolerite. The owners, James and Karen Stewart, transformed what was locally known as "misery farm" into a thriving vineyard, overcoming the initial perception that the land was unsuitable for growing. The name "Caledon" itself means "rocky hill," fitting the terrain. The cellar door is architecturally designed by Cumulus Studio and features a five-metre-high stone wall crafted from sandstone excavated directly from the site. The vineyard was planted with careful consideration for the existing native gum trees, removing only one tree during the process. The property also hosts community events, such as the "Caledon Crawl," an 850-metre race up the vineyard driveway that raises funds for Landcare Tasmania. The winery is family-owned and operated, with a strong focus on reflecting the unique terroir of their single site in their wines.
