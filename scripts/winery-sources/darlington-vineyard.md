## Founding & History
Darlington Vineyard was established in the early 1990s, with its vines planted during this period. The vineyard derives its name from the historic settlement of Darlington on nearby Maria Island, which was the site of an earlier vineyard experiment in 1885 that was subsequently abandoned a decade later. In late 2021, Janice and Dale Evans became the new owners of Darlington Vineyard.

## Vineyard & Site
Darlington Vineyard is situated on a two-hectare site, located approximately one kilometer from the sheltered waters of Prosser Bay on Tasmania's East Coast. The vineyard features 5,000 mature vines, which were planted in the early 1990s and are now approximately 30 years old. The rows are oriented north-east on a sloping block, a design that facilitates the drainage of cool air from the vineyard's lower sections, thereby mitigating the effects of frost. The site benefits from the warm, sunny east coast summers, allowing fruit to ripen beautifully, with picking typically occurring in April. The vineyard's environment is characterized by a cool climate, dry east coast conditions, and "hungry soil". Four varietals are planted: Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Riesling, and Sauvignon Blanc. The farming approach emphasizes personal attention to each vine, with hand-tending and hand-picking ensuring care. Low cropping rates are maintained to produce small berries with intense flavors. The trellising method used is vertical shoot positioning, with an aim to produce two kilograms of fruit from each plant.
