## Founding & History
Derwent Estate Vineyard was established in 1992 when Trevor and Pat Hanigan planted their first vines on the historic Mount Nassau property. The Hanigan family, representing five generations, had farmed the land at Granton since 1913, engaging in sheep and cattle farming, growing vegetables, seed crops, poppies, and quarrying for lime. The property itself dates back to the 1820s. The family began their own winery operations in 2014, after initially selling much of their fruit to other companies like Penfolds, McWilliam's, and Accolade Wines. The new 600 square meter straw-bale winery and barrel room was officially opened in 2014, coinciding with experienced winemaker John Schuts joining as a business partner.

## People
The current owners of Derwent Estate are the Hanigan family, who have farmed the land for over a century. Andrew Hanigan is the viticulturist and represents the fifth generation of the family business. He has been involved with the vineyard since he was 13 years old. John Schuts is the principal winemaker and a partner in the business, having joined in 2014. Schuts previously worked at Winemaking Tasmania for 11 years, where he recognized the quality of Derwent Estate's grapes. General Manager Ange Morgan joined the Derwent Estate team in 2019.

## Vineyard & Site
Derwent Estate Vineyard is located on north-facing slopes that run down to the Derwent River, an aspect suitable for cool-climate grape production. The vineyard is situated at sea level in the lower Derwent Valley. The property has grown in stages and currently spans 20 hectares, with five different grape varieties planted across two sites. Other sources indicate the vineyard area as 10.75 hectares. The soils are described as permeable loams over limestone, which is a rare calcareous outcrop in Tasmania. This limestone originates from a 250-280 million-year-old fossilized seabed. The cool climate and calcareous soil contribute to high acidity in Chardonnay grapes.

Varietals planted include Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Riesling, Sauvignon Blanc, and a Shiraz-Nebbiolo blend. The Chardonnay vineyard is 28 years old. Pinot Noir vines are grown on VSP (Vertical Shoot Positioned) trellising and include six different clones. The farming approach involves minimal intervention, avoiding insecticides and invasive pesticides whenever possible. Derwent Estate has been using organic viticultural practices for about seven years. Vines are meticulously hand-tended, and yields are reduced to one bunch per shoot for some wines.

## Cellar Door Experience
The Cellar Door at Derwent Estate is housed in the historic Rathbone Cottage, which dates back to 1820 and was built by convicts. It offers a tasting experience of 12 different varietals. Visitors can sit by the fire indoors or on the verandah overlooking the Derwent River. Bookings are recommended for all tasting experiences, especially for groups, to ensure a personalized visit. The Cellar Door is open daily from 10 am to 5 pm, except for Christmas Day and Boxing Day. Some sources state it is open Thursday-Monday 11 am-5 pm or Monday-Friday 10 am-4 pm (with Sundays in December and January).

## Food & Hospitality
Derwent Estate features "The Shed" Restaurant, which offers lunches crafted from local Tasmanian produce, paired with Derwent Estate wines. The restaurant operates Wednesday to Sunday. Kitchen operating hours are 12 pm to 3 pm on Wednesdays and Thursdays, and 11:30 am to 3 pm on Fridays, Saturdays, and Sundays, with the venue closing at 5 pm. The dining space was transformed from an original shearing and tractor shed, blending rustic and contemporary styles. The tables in The Shed are built with timber from the original shearing shed. The estate is suitable for functions.

## Awards & Recognition
Derwent Estate's Calcaire Chardonnay 2011 scored 97 points and was named best Tasmanian Chardonnay at the 2013 James Halliday Chardonnay Challenge. The Calcaire Chardonnays have consistently scooped gold medals and trophies at the Tasmanian regional wine show. Derwent Estate was acclaimed for its premier Pinot Noir, winning the producer of the year title in 2011. James Halliday notes that the grapes retained by Derwent Estate have produced consistently exceptional wines. Their 2023 Chardonnay was a trophy winner at the Tasmanian Wine Show.
