## Founding & History
The Devil's Corner vineyard was first planted in 2005. Brown Brothers acquired the vineyard site in 2010. The current Cellar Door and Lookout, designed by Cumulus Studio, reopened in December 2015, replacing a smaller, temporary structure. An impressive site renovation was undertaken in 2021. The name "Devil's Corner" originates from a treacherous bend in the Tasmania Tamar River, discovered by sailors.

## People
Devil's Corner is part of the Brown Family Wine Group, which also includes Brown Brothers, Tamar Ridge, Pirie, and Innocent Bystander. Tom Wallace is the Senior Winemaker for Devil's Corner, Tamar Ridge, and Pirie Sparkling. Brett McClen oversees viticultural operations, focusing on soil health and water conservation across the Brown Family Wine Group portfolio.

## Vineyard & Site
The Devil's Corner vineyard is located on Tasmania's East Coast, overlooking Moulting Lagoon and the Hazards mountain range. It is Tasmania's largest vineyard, with over 190 hectares (191.5 hectares specifically) under vine. The total property spans 1,500 hectares. The vineyard was first planted in 2005, with subsequent plantings increasing its size.

The soils are predominantly shallow silt loams and gravels, with heavier clays underneath, which promote moderate vine vigor and an optimal balance between canopy size and fruit yields. The site benefits from a cool maritime climate, influenced by prevailing winds off the southern ocean, which allows for slow grape development, retention of aromatics, fruit, and acidity. This maritime influence also reduces frost risk and diurnal temperature variation, aiding in gradual acid decline at ripening and preserving delicate fruit flavors. The region experiences calm autumns, wild winters, and warm summers.

Varietals planted include Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc, Riesling, and Syrah (Shiraz). Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, and aromatic whites dominate the classic varietal mix. The vineyard is divided into 35 individually managed blocks, with viticulture tailored to each block's nature and the desired wine style.

## Cellar Door Experience
The Cellar Door is open daily from 10 am to 5 pm, closing only on Christmas Day and Good Friday. It features a timber-clad tower and lookout offering panoramic views of the Freycinet Peninsula, Moulting Lagoon, and the Hazards Vineyard. The design, which underwent a significant renovation in 2015 and 2021, uses timber-clad shipping containers to frame views and provide shelter.

Visitors can enjoy wine tastings, including a "Taste at your own Pace" option with premium handcrafted tasting paddles for up to 5 wines at $20 per person, available hourly or as walk-ins in the Hazards Tasting Room. Premium hosted tastings are also available for $50 per person, offering guided tastings of premium wines. An exclusive Wine & Chocolate Tasting is offered for $35 per person, pairing four wines with artisanal delights. Private bookings and bespoke events can be held in the atmospheric underground cellar, "The Devil's Den". No bookings are required for general bar service.

## Food & Hospitality
Devil's Corner hosts its own Devil's Corner Kitchen, which opened in November 2025, focusing on local produce, smokehouse flavors, and garden-to-plate principles. The kitchen is open Thursday-Monday from 11 am to 4 pm, closed on Christmas Day, Good Friday, Tuesdays, and Wednesdays. The menu, designed by Head Chef Jack Bennett, includes wood-fired pizzas, handmade pastas, and seasonal mains, with suggested wine pairings. Examples from the launch menu include Chardonnay-braised Tasmanian pork belly pizza, duck and mushroom pizza, venison pappardelle, beef short rib, and wallaby and Pinot Noir Bolognese.

In addition to Devil's Corner Kitchen, the cellar door partners with local East Coast foodies Fishers of Freycinet and Tombolo Freycinet. Fishers of Freycinet offers fresh local seafood, including oysters, beer-battered fish and chips, and chili mussels, open daily from 10 am to 4 pm (hot food until 3:30 pm). Tombolo Freycinet provides wood-fired pizzas. Visitors can also create their own cheese boards.

The venue can host private functions and bespoke events.

## Sustainability & Certifications
Devil's Corner is committed to sustainability, focusing on building soil health and water conservation. Their vineyards and winery were certified sustainable by Sustainable Winegrowers Australia (SWA) in 2023. Practices include treating each of the 35 vineyard blocks individually, implementing nutrition programs, irrigation, pruning, and canopy management tailored to specific blocks. They utilize 5,000 sheep for weed suppression and to build soil health. The winery has also implemented a water treatment plant utilizing dam water for the entire vineyard and cellar door site. They record energy and water usage to assess environmental impact and identify improvement opportunities.

## Notable / Distinctive
Devil's Corner Cellar Door is an iconic destination on Tasmania's East Coast, known for its striking architectural design featuring a timber-clad tower and lookout crafted from shipping containers, offering panoramic views of Moulting Lagoon and the Hazards mountain range. This design was part of a significant renovation in 2015, aiming to create a unique tourism experience. The winery's location, where "vines meet the wild blue sea," is considered wild, rugged, and remote, yet wildly rewarding.

The winemaking philosophy embraces the challenging maritime climate, with winemakers like Tom Wallace viewing the "battle with nature" as fantastic, leading to different wines each year and the "excitement of imperfection". They "lean into" the elements rather than fighting them, harnessing the chaos to create their wines. The property is Tasmania's largest vineyard, yet viticultural operations treat its 35 blocks individually, maintaining a "small vineyard mindset". The integration of local food partners like Fishers of Freycinet and Tombolo Freycinet, alongside their own Devil's Corner Kitchen, provides a comprehensive food and wine experience that celebrates local produce and pairings.
