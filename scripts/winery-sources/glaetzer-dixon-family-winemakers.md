## Founding & History

Glaetzer-Dixon Family Winemakers was established in 2008 by Nick Glaetzer and his wife, Sally Dixon. Nick Glaetzer comes from a prominent Barossa Valley winemaking family, with roots dating back to 1888 when his great-great-grandparents emigrated to the region. His father, Colin Glaetzer, founded Glaetzer Wines in the Barossa Valley. Nick Glaetzer moved to Tasmania in 2005, driven by an interest in cool-climate Shiraz and Pinot Noir. He initially worked at Frogmore Creek and Moorilla, sharing winemaking facilities, before establishing Glaetzer-Dixon in a converted ice factory on the edge of Hobart's CBD. This urban winery and tasting room is located in Glebe, approximately 500m from central Hobart. A significant milestone for the winery was in 2011 when its 2010 Mon Père Shiraz became the first Tasmanian wine to win the prestigious Jimmy Watson Memorial Trophy at the Royal Melbourne Wine Show.

## People

The winery is owned and operated by winemaker Nick Glaetzer and his wife, Sally Dixon (née Dixon), who is a former journalist. Nick Glaetzer is a sixth-generation vigneron. He gained extensive experience working at various wineries globally, including Leeuwin Estate, Wolf Blass, Lindeman's, Rosemount, Evans & Tate, and Frogmore Creek in Australia, as well as Domaine de la Ferrrandière in Languedoc, Domaine Albert Morot in Burgundy, and Weingut Egon Müller in Germany. His international experiences, particularly in Burgundy, fostered a passion for Pinot Noir and Riesling, ultimately leading him to Tasmania.

## Vineyard & Site

Glaetzer-Dixon Family Winemakers operates as an urban winery in Hobart, sourcing grapes from various Tasmanian growers. They primarily source fruit from around a dozen growers across Tasmania, including the Tamar, Derwent, and Coal River Valleys. Some of these vineyards were established by pioneers of the Tasmanian wine industry in the early 1970s. Nick Glaetzer has also planted his own 12-hectare vineyard at Tea Tree in the Coal River Valley. The first harvest from this estate vineyard was anticipated in 2021, though other sources indicate 2022 or 2024. This new vineyard includes Barossa Old Vine cuttings from a 100-year-old Barossa vineyard.
