## Founding & History
Home Hill Winery was established in 1992 by Terry and Rosemary Bennett in the Huon Valley, south of Hobart, Tasmania. It began as a hobby farm with six rows of vines and has since grown into an award-winning vineyard. The first vintage was produced in 1998. The name "Home Hill" originated from a town Terry and Rosemary drove through between Cairns and Noosa, and also reflects their family home situated on a hill above the vineyard. The winery and restaurant, featuring an architecturally designed rammed earth building, developed from this initial hobby.

## People
Home Hill Winery is a family-owned and operated business. The proprietors are Terry and Rosemary Bennett. Their youngest son, Sean Bennett, serves as the Vineyard Manager, assisting Terry with vineyard planning and cropping levels. Their daughter, Kelly Bennett, manages the Wine Club and other duties. Rosemary's sister, Gail, is the Office & Function Manager, and her niece, Prue, handles PR & Marketing. The winemaker is Catalina Collado.

## Vineyard & Site
The vineyard was initially planted in 1992 with 0.5 hectares of Pinot Noir. Between 1994 and 1999, an additional 5 hectares were established, with Pinot Noir remaining the primary focus. The vineyard has gradually expanded to over 6 hectares, and other sources indicate it has grown to 10.2 hectares or 12 hectares. The vineyard's soil is described as grey loam with a high clay content, and it benefits from reliable rainfall. It is situated near the 43rd degree latitude, similar to Bordeaux in France, which contributes to a cool climate with slow fruit ripening during warmer months. This climate, characterized by winter fog, frost, and spring rain, is ideal for producing cool-climate wines. The primary varietals planted are Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, and Sylvaner. Pinot Noir plantings include Davis clones 2051 (D5V12) and 8104 (D2V6), with small plantings of modern Burgundy clones 114 and 115 also present. The winery has implemented an irrigation system to mitigate the impact of severe spring frosts common in the Huon Valley.

## Cellar Door Experience
The cellar door at Home Hill Winery is open daily from 10 am to 5 pm. Some sources indicate hours from 11 am to 4 pm. Bookings are not essential for groups under six people, but are recommended for larger groups. Visitors can enjoy five tastings of their choice for $10. The cellar door offers a relaxed and welcoming tasting experience with views over the vineyard and surrounding countryside. Guests can sample the full range of wines, guided by a knowledgeable team. The setting is described as having rustic charm, complemented by the hospitality of the Bennett family. The cellar door is fully accessible.

## Awards & Recognition
Home Hill Winery has received significant awards and recognition. They won the prestigious Jimmy Watson Memorial Trophy at the Royal Melbourne Wine Show in 2015 for their 2014 Kelly's Reserve Pinot Noir. This wine also earned the James Halliday trophy at the same show. The winery has consistently won multi-trophies and gold medals at the Tasmanian Wine Show. They achieved international success at the Tri Nations Wine Show in 2006, winning Best Pinot Noir for their 2005 vintage. Home Hill was named Vineyard of the Year in 2015. ## Notable / Distinctive
Home Hill Winery is particularly distinctive for its success with Pinot Noir, notably winning the Jimmy Watson Memorial Trophy for its 2014 Kelly's Reserve Pinot Noir, considered one of Australia's most coveted wine awards. The winery is a family-owned and run operation, with multiple family members involved in various aspects of the business. The site was originally an apple orchard before being converted to a vineyard. The architectural design of the rammed earth restaurant and cellar door is also a notable feature. The winery offers unique experiences such as yoga sessions followed by brunch and bubbles. A challenge faced by the winery is the severe frosts in the Huon Valley, which they address with an irrigation system. Another challenge is that their wine production is not always large enough to meet demand, leading them to prioritize local suppliers and cellar door sales.
