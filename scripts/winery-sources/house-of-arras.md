## Founding & History
House of Arras was established in 1995 under the ownership of BRL Hardy, which is now Accolade Wines. The winery was founded with the specific goal of producing traditional-method sparkling wine from Tasmanian fruit that could compete with the finest Champagnes. Chief winemaker Ed Carr has led the brand since its inception.

In October 2023, Handpicked Wines acquired House of Arras from Accolade Wines. This acquisition included the brand, inventory, 24 hectares of premium vineyard, the winery, and the Pipers River cellar door. Accolade Wines continues to produce and bottle House of Arras wines under contract for the new owners. The name "Arras" signifies a "rich tapestry," reflecting the diverse vineyard sources across Tasmania that contribute to its wines. The first Arras wine, a Grand Vintage, was released in 1999.

## People
Ed Carr is the chief winemaker and has been with House of Arras since its founding in 1995. He is recognized as Australia's most awarded sparkling winemaker, having amassed over 100 trophies in Australian wine shows, including 21 consecutive "Best Sparkling White Wine of Show" trophies. Carr's vision for Arras focused on extended lees aging, a practice common in Champagne, with some prestige wines aging for a decade or more. In 2018, he received the Lifetime Achievement Award at the Champagne & Sparkling Wine World Championships, the only non-Champenois winemaker to achieve this honor. In 2024, he was also awarded “Sparkling Winemaker of the Year” at the International Wine Challenge.

The current owner is Handpicked Wines, a family-owned company led by William Dong, who is the CEO and Founder. The core winemaking and vineyard teams, including Ed Carr, remained in place after the acquisition by Handpicked Wines. Ben Bussell is noted as the Viticulturalist.

## Vineyard & Site
House of Arras sources Chardonnay, Pinot Noir, and Pinot Meunier from leading Tasmanian sparkling base sub-regions. The total vineyard area is approximately 120 hectares, a combination of owned, leased, and contract supply. The winery's own vineyards cover 24 hectares. Grapes are sourced from six distinct regions across Tasmania: Pipers River, Tamar Valley, Coal River Valley, East Coast, Huon Valley, and Derwent Valley. Each vineyard block is managed with sparkling wine production in mind, and only the finest fruit is selected. Tasmania's cool climate, maritime influence, and ancient soils are considered ideal for producing high-acid, finely textured fruit suitable for sparkling wine. The Pipers River region, where the cellar door is located, is Tasmania's northernmost wine region.

## Awards & Recognition
House of Arras is Australia's most awarded traditional-method sparkling wine producer. It has won multiple Decanter World Wine Awards trophies as the best sparkling wine outside Champagne over the past two decades. James Halliday has consistently awarded House of Arras top scores, and the brand holds 5 Red Stars in the Halliday Wine Companion.

In 2024, House of Arras achieved significant international recognition at the Champagne & Sparkling Wine World Championships (CSWWC), securing 6 of the 7 gold medals awarded to Australian wines and 8 of the 15 silver medals. Specific medal-winning wines in 2024 included the 2015 Blanc de Blancs, 2005 Museum Release Blanc de Blancs, 2016 Grand Vintage, 2014 Grand Vintage, 2008 E.J Carr Late Disgorged, 2005 E.J Carr Late Disgorged, and 2015 Vintage Rosé. The brand also won the Chairman's Trophy at the 2024 CSWWC and a Platinum Medal at the 2024 Decanter World Wine Awards.

House of Arras was awarded Australia's first-ever 100-point rating for a sparkling wine by the Robert Parker Wine Advocate for its E.J. Carr Late Disgorged 1998 Magnum. This was also the first time a sparkling wine produced outside France's Champagne region received a 100-point rating from this publication. The brand was also named Decanter's Sparkling Wine of the Year in 2020.
