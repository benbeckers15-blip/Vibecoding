## Founding & History
Josef Chromy Wines was founded by Josef Chromy, who arrived in Tasmania in 1950 as a 19-year-old refugee from Czechoslovakia. After building a successful meat and smallgoods business, Blue Ribbon Meat Products, he ventured into the wine industry. Over thirty years, from the age of 63, Josef Chromy developed several Tasmanian wineries including Rochecombe (now Bay of Fires), Heemskerk, Jansz, and Tamar Ridge Wines. He purchased the 61-hectare vineyard at Relbia in 2003. Josef Chromy Wines officially launched in December 2007, when Josef was 76 years old. Some sources indicate the business was established in 2004, which may refer to the vineyard acquisition and initial development. The estate quickly gained recognition, securing 14 trophies and 170 medals within its first four years. In April 2022, Josef Chromy Wines was acquired by Paragon Wine Estates, a part of Endeavour Group.

## People
The winery was founded by Josef Chromy OAM. The current Chief Winemaker is Ockie Myburgh. Yael Sandler is also a winemaker, having joined the team for vintage in 2016 and becoming assistant winemaker before taking on the winemaker role. Jeremy Dineen served as chief winemaker for 15 years, departing in 2021. Key staff also include Kellie Graham as Viticulture Manager Tasmania, Nick Raitt as Executive Chef, Dave Milne as Sales Manager, Export & Trade, and Donovan Stull as Direct to Consumer Marketing Specialist.

## Vineyard & Site
Josef Chromy Wines is situated in Relbia, south of Launceston, within the Tamar Valley wine region of Northern Tasmania. The primary vineyard spans 61 hectares. An additional 17-hectare vineyard in the Coal Valley in southern Tasmania was recently acquired, broadening the estate's diversity. The vineyard lies at an altitude between 81 and 170 metres and benefits from a temperate maritime climate. The soils are described as rich alkaline clay and stony, resting atop an ancient dolerite base. The vineyard cultivates cool-climate varietals including Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Riesling, Pinot Gris, Sauvignon Blanc, and Pinot Meunier. The farming approach emphasizes minimal impact viticulture and sustainable practices, aligning with the standards of Sustainable Winegrowing Australia (SWA). This includes an Integrated Pest Management (IPM) system, a Soft Fungicide Program, and in-house composting of grape marc and other by-products.

## Cellar Door Experience
The cellar door is housed in the original 1880s homestead, surrounded by old English gardens and overlooking a picturesque lake and vineyard. It is open daily from 10 am to 5 pm, closing only on Christmas Day. Last wine tastings commence at 4:00 pm. Visitors can enjoy a flight of four wines, with bookings recommended for all tasting experiences to ensure a personalized visit. Distinctive experiences include a guided estate tour and the "Art of Sparkling" masterclass, where guests can participate in dosage trials to craft their own sparkling wine and receive a take-home bottle. The winery also offers a "Go Behind the Label" tour that delves into the winemaking process.

## Food & Hospitality
Josef Chromy Wines features an award-winning restaurant that offers à la carte lunches. The menu focuses on showcasing the finest local Tasmanian produce, with dishes often paired with estate wines. Executive Chef Nick Raitt leads the kitchen, emphasizing quality and freshness by forging connections with local growers and suppliers. The restaurant provides fine dining with views over the estate lake. Dining is available seven days over summer and Thursday to Monday from May to September. The estate also serves as a venue for weddings, corporate functions, and private events. Notable events hosted include the Effervescence Tasmania sparkling wine festival and the Great Chefs Series.

## Awards & Recognition
Josef Chromy Wines has received numerous accolades since its launch, including 14 trophies and 170 medals within its first four years. Some sources report over 17 trophies and 200 medals. The winery earned a five-star rating (top rating) in James Halliday's 2014 Australian Wine Companion. Specific awards include the Trophy for Best Chardonnay at the Decanter World Wine Awards in London in 2013 for the 2011 Josef Chromy Estate Chardonnay. Other awards include the Trophy for Best Riesling at the National Cool Climate Wine Show 2022 for the Josef Chromy Estate Riesling 2021, the Trophy for Best Pinot Noir at the Sydney Royal Wine Show 2019 for the Josef Chromy Estate Pinot Noir 2017, and the Trophy for Best Sparkling (24-60 months tirage) at the Australian Sparkling Wine Show for the Josef Chromy Vintage Sparkling. The restaurant has been awarded One Chef Hat every year since 2017. Both the restaurant and cellar door have been inducted into the Tasmanian Tourism Awards Hall of Fame.

## Sustainability & Certifications
Josef Chromy Wines is dedicated to sustainable farming and is aligned with the standards set by Sustainable Winegrowing Australia (SWA). Key sustainability practices include a 100kW solar power system, advanced wastewater treatment, and extensive composting of grape marc and other winery by-products, which are then returned to the soil. The vineyard employs an Integrated Pest Management (IPM) system and a Soft Fungicide Program, focusing on bolstering beneficial insect populations rather than relying solely on insecticides. Other practices include minimizing soil compaction, maintaining buffer zones for natural predators, and a grass "sword" between rows to prevent soil erosion. The winery also has a state-of-the-art water purification plant that recycles all water for vineyard use, and grape marc is processed into cattle feed. Josef Chromy Wines was also the first Tasmanian winery to utilize Lean+Green™ wine bottles, which are lighter and more environmentally friendly.

## Notable / Distinctive
Josef Chromy's life story is a distinctive aspect of the winery, having arrived in Australia as a penniless refugee and subsequently becoming a significant figure in the Tasmanian food and wine industry. He was instrumental in developing several prominent Tasmanian wineries before establishing his namesake estate. The winery is capable of producing, aging, and disgorging traditional method sparkling wine entirely on the estate. A unique offering is "The Art of Sparkling" masterclass, which allows visitors a hands-on experience in crafting their own sparkling wine, including dosage trials, and taking a bottle home. The cellar door is set within an original 1880s homestead, surrounded by historic gardens and a lake. The winery's commitment to sustainability is notable, including its early adoption of Lean+Green™ wine bottles in Tasmania. Josef Chromy was recognized with an OAM in 1997 for his contributions to the meat industry and later upgraded to an AO in 2023 for his broader impact on wineries, tourism, property development, and community support.
