## Founding & History
Moorilla Winery was established in 1958 by Italian immigrant and textile merchant Claudio Alcorso, who planted the first grapevines on a small peninsula on the River Derwent in Tasmania. Some sources indicate the founding year as 1956. Alcorso was a pioneer in Tasmanian winemaking, daring to cultivate fine wines in a cool climate. He also founded the Australian brand Sheridan. Moorilla was one of Tasmania's first wineries, and some sources state it was the second longest continuously operating commercial vineyard in Tasmania.

Alcorso remained at the helm for over 30 years. In 1993, he acquired a second vineyard, St Matthias, near Launceston. The winery faced financial difficulties a few years later. In 1995, David Walsh, a professional gambler and art collector, purchased Moorilla. Walsh initially bought the property for the Roy Grounds house, which became the entrance to the Museum of Old and New Art (MONA), rather than for the wine.

As part of the development of MONA, the winery was completely rebuilt and is now situated just above the museum entrance. In 2018, Walsh expanded Moorilla's legacy by purchasing Domaine A wine estate, the oldest in Tasmania's Coal River Valley.

## People
The current owner of Moorilla Winery is David Walsh, who acquired the property in 1995. Walsh is also the creator of the Museum of Old and New Art (MONA).

The principal winemaker is Conor van der Reest, a Canadian expat who was recruited by David Walsh and given free rein at Moorilla. Van der Reest has experience in winemaking from various international locations, including Ontario's Niagara Peninsula, Languedoc, Champagne, and Australia's prime wine country. He redefined the winery with a focus on its terroir, an ambitious cellaring program, and an overhaul of facilities. He also leads winemaking for Domaine A, which Moorilla acquired in 2018.

## Vineyard & Site
Moorilla's wines are produced from two vineyards: the original Moorilla vineyard, located on Claudio Alcorso's initial site next to the Derwent River, which it shares with MONA, and the St Matthias vineyard on the west bank of the Tamar River, 15 kilometers north of Launceston. The Moorilla vineyard is situated on Frying Pan Island, a small peninsula in the Derwent River, approximately one meter above sea level. The original vineyard was planted in 1958 with Riesling cuttings. The site's river breeze and soil diversity contribute to a long ripening process. The Moorilla vineyard is 2 hectares.

The vineyard soils at Moorilla are described as wind-strewn silty clay and sand. In 1979, Moorilla became the first commercial vineyard in Australia to adopt Professor Alain Carbonneau's innovative 'lyre system' of vineyard trellising. This U-shaped trellising supported new clones of Pinot Noir, including D5V12 (2051), MV6, and the Swiss Wädenswil and Mariafeld selections.

Moorilla's farming approach in both its Frying Pan Island and St Matthias vineyards is sustainable, incorporating both organic and biodynamic techniques.

## Cellar Door Experience
Moorilla's Cellar Door is located on the property, housed in a building alongside MONA's main restaurant. The Cellar Door is open from 12 pm to 5 pm, Friday to Monday, and is closed on Christmas Day. Visitors can experience tastings and a range of premium tours and experiences through the Ultimate Winery Experiences Australia program. The tasting room features "living tables" planted with plants and herbs, allowing guests to dine and drink among the greenery.

## Food & Hospitality

## Notable / Distinctive
Moorilla Winery is distinctive due to its direct association with the Museum of Old and New Art (MONA), owned by David Walsh. Walsh initially bought the property for an art warehouse, not for the wine, and later became enamored with the winemaking lifestyle. The winery was completely rebuilt as part of MONA's development. This connection means the winery is often described as "a winery with a museum attached". The property also includes a brewery, Moo Brew, which produces five core beers and seasonal varieties. Moorilla's approach to wine, influenced by David Walsh's philosophy for MONA, is described as challenging, uncompromising, and deeply intelligent, aiming for wines that encourage thought and discovery rather than easy consumption. The tasting room features unique "living tables" where guests can dine and drink among plants.
