## Founding & History
Nandroya Vineyard was first planted in the early 1990s by John Rees on slopes above the North West Bay River in Margate, Tasmania. The vineyard was established on land that previously supported apple trees. While some sources state the vineyard was established in 1990, another indicates 1995. The name 'Nandroya' is derived from an Aboriginal phrase meaning 'a family living in harmony'. John Rees opened a cellar door approximately 15 years prior to 2024 to share his passion for wine with friends and visitors. Following John Rees's passing, the Nandroya family continues to operate the vineyard and welcome visitors.

## People
The vineyard was founded by John Rees and his family. Vignerons John and Joy Rees are noted for their approach to winemaking, reflecting the meaning of 'Nandroya'. The Nandroya family continues to run the vineyard. Di Aldous is the expert winemaker for Nandroya, having studied winemaking at Melbourne University. Her background as a pharmacist instilled in her a focus on precision, art, science, and communication. Di Aldous aims to produce quality, affordable wines and works closely with the grape grower to extract the best characteristics from the grapes with minimal processing. Sam Rees is also involved, managing the vineyard for the 2024 Pinot Noir vintage.

## Vineyard & Site
Nandroya Vineyard is located in Margate, within the Huon/Channel wine region of Tasmania. It spans 1.5 hectares (approximately 3.7 to 4 acres). The vineyard is slightly elevated and faces northeast, optimizing sun exposure during the growing season. Its location above the North West Bay River provides views of Kunanyi/Mt Wellington to the west and the D'Entrecasteaux Channel to the east. The site experiences warm days and cool nights, characteristic of a cool-climate vineyard. Vintage can extend as late as May, coinciding with snow on nearby Mt Wellington. The primary varietals planted are Pinot Noir and Sauvignon Blanc. Sauvignon Blanc was planted in 1990 and has since become their signature wine. The vineyard's philosophy is founded on relationships, extending to the interdependence of soil, aspect, and the organic principles used in vineyard management.
