## Founding & History
Sailor Seeks Horse was established in 2010 by Gilli and Paul Lipscombe. The couple met in London in 2005 and decided to pursue a career in wine, working vintages in the Languedoc, Margaret River, Oregon, and New Zealand while studying oenology and viticulture. They specifically sought a cool-climate region in Australia to produce Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, eventually settling on the Huon Valley in Tasmania.

They purchased a 6.5-hectare vineyard in Cradoc, Huon Valley, in 2010, which had been originally planted in 2005 but was subsequently abandoned by its previous owners. The vineyard was in a dilapidated state, with half the vines dead and overgrown with blackberry bushes. Despite the significant challenges, a viticultural liaison officer recognized the site's potential, stating it was "definitely worth it". The Lipscombes undertook the extensive work of rehabilitating the vineyard. The name "Sailor Seeks Horse" originated from a handwritten sign in a local café in Cygnet, which resonated with the couple's journey of pursuing a challenging dream with limited resources. Their first wine release was the 2013 Pinot Noir.

## People
Sailor Seeks Horse is owned and operated by Gilli and Paul Lipscombe, who are also the principal winemakers. Paul is from Essex, England, and Gilli is from Brisbane, Australia; they met in London in 2004. They both hold degrees in winemaking and viticulture, having studied in Western Australia. Prior to establishing Sailor Seeks Horse, they gained extensive experience at various wineries, including Xanadu and Devil's Lair in Margaret River, Beaux Freres and Chehalem in Oregon, and Mt Difficulty in Central Otago. Since 2011, they have also been the winemakers for Home Hill Winery in the Huon Valley, where they won the prestigious Jimmy Watson Trophy in 2015. Gilli and Paul Lipscombe were recognized as the Young Gun of Wine Winemaker's Choice winners in 2018.

## Vineyard & Site
The Sailor Seeks Horse vineyard is located in Cradoc, within Tasmania's Huon Valley, which is described as Australia's southernmost municipality and coolest wine region. The vineyard spans 6.5 hectares. It is situated on a steep, north-east facing slope, which provides shelter from prevailing south-westerly winds and is largely frost-free. The soil consists of free-draining quartz-inflected mudstone over clay, or sandy-loam soil over clay, derived from sandstone-derived Permian mudstone. The soils are described as infertile and acidic, which the Lipscombes consider an advantage for producing wines with restraint and site expression.

The vineyard was initially planted in 2005. When the Lipscombes acquired it in 2010, it contained Pinot Noir, Sauvignon Blanc, Sangiovese, and Nebbiolo. They removed the Sauvignon Blanc, Sangiovese, and Nebbiolo, as these varieties were deemed unsuitable for the region, along with some drought-affected Pinot Noir. They then planted 2 hectares of Chardonnay, using Dijon clones 277, 76, 95, and 96. The remaining Pinot Noir plantings include clones 114, 115, MV6, 777, 2051 (D5V12), and 8048 (D2V6). In 2018, a newer block was planted with clones favoring purity and lifted aromatics for a Pinot Noir cuvée named Huldufólk. In 2019, new plantings of Trousseau were made on a steep north-west facing slope.

The vineyard is planted at a density of 4000 vines per hectare. The farming approach is sustainable, with a strong desire to farm organically, and vines are farmed without synthetic additions. The vineyard is primarily dry-farmed, relying on approximately 700mm of average annual rainfall, though new plantings receive some water in their first couple of years. Undervine mulching is used during arid summers. The Lipscombes aim to drive the vines' roots deep into the clay to make them self-sufficient, believing that not irrigating allows the site and vintage variations to express themselves fully.

## Awards & Recognition
Paul and Gilli Lipscombe won the Jimmy Watson Trophy in 2015 for their work as winemakers at Home Hill Winery. They were also awarded the Young Gun of Wine Winemaker's Choice in 2018. Their wines have garnered high praise, with the 2022 Sailor Seeks Horse Pinot Noir receiving a score of 97 from James Suckling and being described as "best ever" by Halliday. The 2024 Sailor Seeks Horse Pinot Noir received a rating of 96/100. Their wines are poured at high-end restaurants in Melbourne and Sydney, including Vue de Monde and Bentley Bar, and sold at respected retailers like Prince Wine Stores. Matthew Jukes, a British wine writer, has also requested samples.

## Sustainability & Certifications
The vineyard is farmed as organically as possible, with a desire to farm organically. Vines are farmed without synthetic additions. The 2024 Small Wonder by Sailor Seeks Horse Chardonnay and Pinot Noir are made from certified organic grapes. The winery practices minimal intervention in winemaking.

## Notable / Distinctive
Sailor Seeks Horse is genuinely distinctive due to its location "on the edge of viticultural possibility" in the Huon Valley, Australia's southernmost and coolest wine region. The founders, Gilli and Paul Lipscombe, intentionally sought a site that was challenging but offered the potential for exceptional Pinot Noir and Chardonnay. Their commitment to rehabilitating a derelict vineyard, described as "the greatest viticultural challenge" by an expert, highlights their dedication. The winery's name itself, derived from a quirky handwritten sign, reflects their "crazy" and determined approach to pursuing their dream. Their winemaking philosophy emphasizes minimal intervention and site expression, aiming for wines with a unique salinity from their quartz-like soils. The Lipscombes' dual role as winemakers for both Sailor Seeks Horse and the Jimmy Watson Trophy-winning Home Hill Winery further underscores their expertise and the high regard for their work.
