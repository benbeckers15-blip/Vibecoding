## Founding & History
Sisu Wines was founded in 2021 by Jake and Mary Sheedy. Jake Sheedy acquired the 95-hectare property, previously a sheep-grazing homestead known as Valley View, in 2019. By 2021, four hectares of vines had been planted. The winery build was completed in March 2025. The architecturally designed cellar door officially opened on December 12, operating throughout the summer from 10:30 am to 4:30 pm. Other sources indicate the cellar door opened in December 2025 or by Christmas 2025, with Halliday Wine Companion also stating 2025. The name "Sisu" is a Finnish concept signifying inner strength, grit, determination, resilience, and fortitude, reflecting the owners' approach to the venture.

## People
Sisu Wines is owned by Jake and Mary Sheedy. Jake Sheedy serves as the principal winemaker and vigneron, as well as the managing director. Prior to establishing Sisu Wines, Jake Sheedy gained experience over 16 vintages, including working alongside Peter Logan at Logan Wines in Mudgee. Jake and Mary Sheedy have a daughter named Lillian.

## Vineyard & Site
The Sisu Wines estate is situated in northern Campania within Tasmania's Coal River Valley, specifically in the Upper Coal River region on Colebrook Road. The property encompasses 95 hectares of gently undulating farmland. Of this, 25 hectares have been planted with vines. The region experiences a cool-temperate climate, influenced by the nearby Southern Ocean, which helps moderate summer heat and provides fresh, dewy nights, ideal for slow and even fruit ripening. The vineyard features a mosaic of three distinct soil structures: black dolerite, red vertosols, and a section of well-draining sandy loam at the base of the hill. Varietals planted include Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Riesling, Pinot Gris, Viognier, Gewürztraminer, Gamay, Syrah (Shiraz), and Nebbiolo. The farming approach is mindful and deeply committed to caring for the land, with a philosophy of treading softly in their craft.

## Cellar Door Experience
Sisu Wines features an architecturally designed cellar door. It opened on December 12, operating throughout the summer from 10:30 am to 4:30 pm. Other sources state it opened in December 2025 and is open Thursday to Monday, while Halliday Wine Companion lists opening hours as 7 days a week from 10:30 am to 4:30 pm. The tasting space is intimate, designed with warm tones and local materials such as Tasmanian blackwood furniture, local sandstone, and spotted gum cladding, and includes a central wood fireplace. Both indoor and outdoor seating are available for unhurried tastings. Visitors can enjoy guided wine flights, as well as wine by the glass or bottle. The cellar door is designed to blend seamlessly with the Coal River landscape. Wine club members are offered exclusive access to winery and vineyard tours.

## Food & Hospitality
The cellar door offers a small, seasonal food selection that highlights local producers. This includes a selection of shared bites. There are plans for future expansion to include a restaurant.

## Awards & Recognition
Sisu Wines' inaugural 2024 estate-grown releases, including Riesling, Chardonnay, and Pinot Noir, have already received recognition from the Halliday Wine Companion. Specific scores include 93 points (silver) for the 2024 SISU Riesling, 91 points (silver) for the 2024 SISU Chardonnay, and 93 points (silver) for the 2024 SISU Pinot Noir. The 2025 SISU Riesling also received 92 points (silver).

## Sustainability & Certifications
The vineyard at Sisu Wines is certified by Sustainable Winegrowing Australia and VinØ. The winery is committed to mindful farming and long-term land stewardship. In partnership with Landcare Tasmania, over 1,300 native trees and shrubs have been planted as part of a long-term commitment to rewild 10% of the farm within five years.

## Notable / Distinctive
Sisu Wines is distinguished as one of the few Tasmanian wineries where all wines are grown, made, and bottled entirely on a single-site estate. The winery utilizes a gravity-fed system. The name "Sisu" itself, a Finnish concept of inner strength and resilience, reflects the challenging journey of establishing the winery. The project involved a significant investment in transforming a former sheep-grazing homestead into a 25-hectare vineyard, complete with a state-of-the-art winery and an architecturally designed cellar door. The background of owner and winemaker Jake Sheedy, who honed his craft over 16 vintages including at Logan Wines, is also notable. Future expansion plans, which included a restaurant and additional vineyard plantings, were temporarily halted due to uncertainties surrounding the Greater South East Irrigation Scheme (GSEIS).
