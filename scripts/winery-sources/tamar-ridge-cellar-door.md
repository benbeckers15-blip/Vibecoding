## Founding & History
Tamar Ridge Wines was established with the first vines planted in 1994, and its inaugural vintage was released in 1999. The winery was initially developed by Tasmanian businessman Josef Chromy. In 1998, following the sale of the Heemskerk Wine Group, the JAC Group appointed Julian Alcorso as chief winemaker and built a new winery on the Kayena vineyard, which had been planted three years prior with the guidance of viticulturist Dr. Richard Smart. The name "Tamar Ridge Wines" was chosen to leverage the Tamar Valley, a significant regional center for commercial vineyards and winemaking in Tasmania.

Tamar Ridge was acquired by Gunns Limited, a Tasmanian timber and agribusiness company, in July 2003. At the time of this sale, Tamar Ridge had already garnered significant recognition, including twelve trophies, twenty gold medals, and numerous silver and bronze medals in Australian wine competitions over four years. James Halliday's Australian Wine Companion awarded Tamar Ridge Wines a five-star rating when the winery was only three years old. In 2010, Tamar Ridge was sold to the Brown Family Wine Group (also referred to as Brown Brothers). This acquisition included the Kayena Vineyard and the Tamar Ridge, Pirie, and Devil's Corner wine brands. The purchase by Brown Brothers was partly driven by a desire to source grapes from cooler regions due to concerns about global warming affecting their Victorian vineyards.

## People
The current owners of Tamar Ridge are the Brown Family Wine Group. Tom Wallace serves as the Chief Winemaker. Viticulturist Ben Pietsch oversees the vineyard operations. Dr. Andrew Pirie, a notable figure in Tasmanian sparkling wine, was previously the chief executive and chief winemaker at Tamar Ridge Estates and retired when Brown Brothers took over in 2010.

## Vineyard & Site
Tamar Ridge's vineyards, primarily the Kayena Vineyard, are located in the rolling basalt and dolerite hills of the Upper Tamar and at White Hills in Tasmania. The Kayena Vineyard, situated on the west bank of the Tamar River, is Tasmania's second-largest by area, spanning over 130 hectares. It was first planted in 1994, with additions over the years expanding it to 134 hectares. The vineyard benefits from a maritime climate, with cooling breezes and moderated temperatures due to its proximity to Bass Strait and the Tamar River, allowing for a long ripening window.

The site features varied aspects and conditions, with steep hillsides rising up to nearly 200m. The soils are distinctive black, self-mulching clay, with differing soil types including clay subsoils and topsoils of quartz sand to clay loam. The altitude of the vineyard ranges from 10m to 30m, with vines planted on northerly and north-easterly undulating slopes.

Varietals planted include Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Riesling, Sauvignon Blanc, and Pinot Gris. Pinot Noir is a strong focus, with almost two dozen clones planted across the vineyard, including clones 15, D4V2, MV6, and 777. The last new plantings occurred in 2018.

The farming approach at Kayena Vineyard incorporates technology for optimizing operations, such as compost applications and irrigation. Viticulturist Ben Pietsch also uses non-technical solutions like roaming poultry and insectary plantings for pest control. All vines are drip irrigated using water collected and stored onsite, with winery wastewater retained and passed through seven gravity-feed ponds before being used for irrigation. Soil moisture tests and evapotranspiration calculations are used to reduce water usage. A permanent mid-row sward is maintained, and ongoing soil testing assesses sequestered carbon, organic matter, and nutrients. Vines are hand-pruned, and much of the harvest is carried out by hand.

## Cellar Door Experience
The Tamar Ridge Cellar Door is open daily from 10 am to 5 pm, closing only on Christmas Day and Good Friday. Winter opening hours may vary, so checking their website and social media is recommended. The cellar door offers views overlooking the Tamar River and mountains.

Tasting experiences are available, with pre-booking recommended, and essential for groups of 8 or more guests. The days of simply dropping in for tastings are largely over in the Tamar Valley, with most wineries requiring bookings. Tasting fees typically range from $10 to $20 per person, depending on the experience.

Tamar Ridge offers several structured tasting experiences:
* **Premium Tamar Tasting**: A flight of three premium wines from their Estate and Reserve ranges.
* **Luxury Tamar Tasting**: A guided tasting of four premium wines, including Estate, Single Block, and Grand Assemblage wines.
* **Ultimate Expressions of Pinot Noir**: Includes five premium wines, encompassing Estate, Reserve, Research, Single Block, and Grand Assemblage wines.
* **Turner Stillhouse and Tamar Ridge Experience**: A joint experience with the neighboring Turner Stillhouse, offering a distillery tour, gin tastings, and guided wine tasting.

The cellar door also provides the opportunity to purchase limited edition, back-vintage Pinots unearthed from the Tamar Ridge Cellar, which are available to taste on select weekends. The cellar door shares its space with PIRIE Tasmania, offering tastings of their sparkling wines.

As of April 2026, the cellar door is undergoing renovations but remains open in a "curated, seasonal format". During this period, tastings and wine sales are hosted on the outdoor terrace, with indoor options at Turner Stillhouse during inclement weather. The renovated cellar door is expected to reopen in June.

## Food & Hospitality
Tamar Ridge offers a shared cheese plate for two as an add-on to tasting experiences, available daily from 12 pm to 3 pm. This platter includes a selection of three Tasmanian cheeses, lavosh crackers, quince paste, fruits, and olives.

The cellar door can be booked for private events. They also host seasonal wine masterclasses, live music on the lawn, roving food vans, and elegant dinners. An annual event called "Noir Noir" is held in collaboration with Turner Stillhouse, celebrating the Winter Solstice with Pinot Noir, whisky, and stargazing, featuring new wine releases and local produce.

## Awards & Recognition
Tamar Ridge Wines achieved significant recognition early in its history, winning twelve trophies, twenty gold medals, and over one hundred bronze medals in Australian wine competitions within its first four years. James Halliday's Australian Wine Companion awarded Tamar Ridge Wines a five-star rating when the winery was only three years old, describing it as "an outstanding winery regularly producing exemplary wines".

## Notable / Distinctive
Tamar Ridge is known for its strong focus on Pinot Noir, with a claim to be "Pinot Noir makers first and wine makers second". The Kayena Vineyard is Tasmania's second-largest by area and boasts significant clonal diversity, with 22 different clones of Pinot Noir planted. The winery is recognized for its site-driven philosophy, where wines reflect the intense, fruity flavors of the vineyard's unique terroir.

A distinctive feature is the sharing of its cellar door with Pirie Tasmania, offering both still and sparkling wine experiences at one location. Additionally, Tamar Ridge collaborates with the neighboring Turner Stillhouse, offering joint wine and spirit tasting experiences. The winery also built Tasmania's first 100% off-grid winery in 2017.
