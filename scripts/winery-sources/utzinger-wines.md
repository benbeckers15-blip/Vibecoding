## Founding & History
Utzinger Wines was established by Lauren and Matthias Utzinger, who planted their vineyard in 2018. The journey began in 2015 when Swiss winemaker Matthias traveled the world seeking an ideal location for a vineyard. He met Lauren, a Tasmanian, and fell in love with her and Northern Tasmania. After two years of searching for an established vineyard that met their criteria, they decided to purchase a block of land and build their own vineyard from scratch. They found their ideal 42-acre, north-east facing block overlooking the Tamar River in August 2017.

## People
The current owners and operators of Utzinger Wines are Lauren and Matthias Utzinger. Matthias Utzinger is the principal winemaker. Matthias has a European winemaking background, including an apprenticeship as a cellar hand in Switzerland and a degree in viticulture and oenology from the University of Changins. His career involved roles in Switzerland, Provence, and the Southern Rhône, and he also worked at Stefano Lubiana's Huon Valley vineyard in Tasmania. Lauren grew up in northern Tasmania. The couple also employs two young winemakers as part-time staff.

## Vineyard & Site
The Utzinger Vineyard spans 5 hectares, with four hectares currently under vine and another hectare in development. Some sources indicate 4 hectares planted. The vineyard was planted in 2018. It is situated on a north-easterly slope in the Tamar Valley, cooled by winds from the kanamaluka/Tamar River, offering a prolonged ripening season. The soil consists of virgin brown dermosols rich with ironstone gravel, described as rich, ferrous topsoil with large volumes of dolerite pebbles over a heavy clay subsoil that acts as a natural sponge for water and nutrients.

Varietals planted include five clones of Pinot Noir, six clones of Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, and Syrah. The vineyard employs high-density planting at 6,500 vines per hectare, with a row spacing of 1.8m, to encourage natural balance and reduce vine vigor. Farming is certified organic since the vineyard's inception. Viticultural practices include a strong focus on canopy management, shoot thinning, and targeted fruit thinning. All fruit is hand-harvested in individual clonal batches. The long-term goal is to dry grow whenever possible, supported by a five-megalitre dam that fills with winter run-off. Utzinger Wines is also preparing to plant three Swiss varieties—petite arvine, rouge du pays, and divico—and Scheurebe, which Matthias imported himself.

## Cellar Door Experience
The Utzinger Wines cellar door is open on Saturdays from 11:00 am to 4:00 pm during vintage. Bookings are required for visits. The cellar door offers a "Seated Wine Flight," a guided tasting for structured small groups of no more than six people (unless a single group). This tasting includes five wines and costs $20 per person, with an option to pair a selection of cheeses from Switzerland and Tasmania. The tasting experience lasts up to 45 minutes.

Visitors can also opt for a "Behind the Scenes" experience, which includes an extended wine flight tasting, a small cheese plate, and time with the winemaker on a tour of the winery and vineyard. This experience is available on Saturdays at 11 am, costs $80 per person, and is limited to a maximum of six people (unless a single group). The cellar door is described as a modern yet inviting space with panoramic vineyard views, where guests can sample wines guided by Lauren and Matthias.

## Awards & Recognition
Utzinger Wines has received notable recognition. Their 'Roter Satz' field blend won the Danger Zone trophy at the 2024 Young Gun of Wine Awards. In the same ceremony, Matthias Utzinger was awarded the Winemaker's Choice trophy. This marks only the third time in the competition's history that a winemaker has won two trophies at the same awards. The wines have also made an immediate impact on The Real Review tasters. Nick Stock, a judge, described the 'Roter Satz' as "dangerously delicious".

Specific Halliday Wine Companion ratings include: Utzinger Estate Black Label Pinot Noir 2024 (95+ gold), Utzinger Roter Satz 2024 (94 silver), and Utzinger Syrah 2024 (95 gold). Utzinger Wines has also been a finalist in the Young Gun of Wine award.

## Sustainability & Certifications
Utzinger Wines is a certified organic vineyard and winery, a certification they have held since their inception. Their approach to viticulture emphasizes creating habitat for beneficial invertebrates and other native wildlife to foster a balanced biosystem. They have dedicated 20% of their land to a revegetation program to create corridors for native wildlife and habitat for beneficial insects and birds. Additionally, 10% of the property is set aside to preserve native grass, and an extra 15% has been planted with trees, understory, and ground cover plants. The vineyard has a five-megalitre dam that collects winter run-off for irrigation, with a long-term goal to dry grow whenever possible. Future plans include band-applying compost under rows to build organic content, support the soil microbiome, and reduce summer soil temperatures.

## Notable / Distinctive
Utzinger Wines stands out due to the Swiss heritage of winemaker Matthias Utzinger, who brings a European winemaking background to Tasmania. A distinctive aspect is their commitment to planting rare Swiss varieties, including petite arvine, rouge du pays, divico, and Scheurebe, which Matthias imported himself. Their 'Roter Satz' field blend, which uniquely combines varieties like Cabernet Sauvignon and Pinot Noir harvested and fermented together, earned the Danger Zone trophy at the 2024 Young Gun of Wine Awards. Matthias Utzinger's achievement of winning both the Danger Zone and Winemaker's Choice trophies at the same Young Gun of Wine Awards in 2024 is a rare feat in the competition's history. The winery was built from a "blank canvas" on land that was previously a sheep and cattle farm and had never seen herbicides or broad-spectrum insecticides, allowing them to establish an organic vineyard from scratch. Their core philosophy, "wine is grown, not made," emphasizes a hands-off, site-expressive winemaking approach. The Utzingers are also noted for being self-sufficient, handling all aspects from soil management to bottling their own wine.
