## Founding & History
Velo Wines, located at the entrance to Tasmania's Tamar Valley, was originally established in 1966 as Legana Estate. The vineyard was founded by Graham Wiltshire, described as a pioneer and icon of the Tasmanian wine industry. Another pioneer, Michael Curtis, is also credited with establishing the vineyard in 1966. The estate is home to some of Tasmania's oldest Cabernet Sauvignon vines, and also Pinot Noir and Shiraz vines.

In 2001, the vineyard was rebranded as Velo Wines by Michael and Mary Wilson. The name "Velo," meaning "bicycle" in French, pays tribute to Michael Wilson's past career as an Olympic and international road cyclist. The Wilsons purchased the established Legana Vineyard in 2001.

In 2017, new owners took over the vineyard, operating under the guidance of Rod Thorpe, former founder of Moore's Hill.

## People
The vineyard was rebranded and owned by Michael and Mary Wilson from 2001. Michael Wilson is a former Olympic cyclist, viticulturist, winemaker, and an association wine judge. ## Vineyard & Site
